Project Overview

A clinical-grade sensor, embedded in a everyday plaster

Chronic wounds — diabetic foot ulcers, pressure injuries, surgical sites — affect millions of patients and carry an enormous economic burden. A sudden, localized rise in temperature is one of the earliest and most reliable signals of inflammation and bacterial colonisation, often preceding redness or pus by several days. This project captures that signal continuously, non-invasively, and at clinical resolution.

The clinical problem

By the time a wound looks infected, the patient is often already days into a deteriorating cascade. Visual inspection also requires removing the dressing — which itself cools the wound bed and tears fragile new tissue.

The thermal signal

A localized temperature rise of more than 2.2 °C over baseline is a definitive early biomarker of impending infection. The TMP117 resolves changes as fine as 0.0078 °C, well below the threshold that matters.

The patch

The sensor is mechanically and thermally integrated across four discrete layers of a flexible plaster, so it reads the heat radiating from the wound — and only the wound — without interference from room air.

The Science

Reading temperature like a clinician

Healing is not a uniform event — it is a moving thermal map. Understanding where heat belongs lets the system tell ordinary healing apart from danger.

The wound thermal spectrum

From the optimal healing window to the unmistakable signature of active infection.

Healthy wound bed

An open bed loses heat by evaporative cooling. Optimal repair happens at 33–35°C; below 33°C, fibroblast activity stalls.

Warm periwound

Intact skin around the wound runs warmer thanks to healthy perfusion — a good prognostic sign when it stays warmer than the bed.

Infection signature

A uniform rise across bed and periwound, or 2–3°C above the matching healthy limb, flags deep microbial colonisation.

① Hemostasis

Within seconds: vasoconstriction and a fibrin–platelet clot seal the wound and recruit immune cells.

② Inflammation

24h–7 days: neutrophils then macrophages clear debris and bacteria — the heat-producing phase.

③ Proliferation

Day 3–21: granulation tissue, new vessels, and keratinocytes migrate inward to rebuild the defect.

④ Maturation

21 days–1 yr: collagen remodels and cross-links, restoring up to 70–80% of original tensile strength.

System Architecture

From heat at the skin to an alert on a screen

The signal chain that turns a fraction of a degree into a clinically actionable warning, built on an ESP32 core with scalable I²C sensing.

01 · SENSE

TMP117

16-bit reading, ±0.1°C, NIST-traceable, EEPROM offset trim.

02 · EXPAND

TCA9548A

8-channel I²C switch lifts the 4-address limit to dozens of sensors.

03 · CARRY

PCA9615 / ESP-NOW

Differential I²C over twisted-pair, or low-power 2.4 GHz wireless.

04 · DECIDE

ESP32

Decodes two's-complement data and checks the +2.2°C alert rule.

05 · LOG

microSD

Time-stamped CSV records on a fault-tolerant FAT32 file system.

Heat flows up; the room is shut out

Stage 1 keeps the substrate beneath the sensor exceptionally thin (~100 µm) so wound heat transfers with minimal thermal lag. Stage 2 places the TMP117 at the exact geometric centre of the patch — directly over the deepest part of the wound — with its thermal pad soldered to a copper/silver pad for a direct path into the silicon die.

Stage 3 coats the sensor in a thermally conductive but electrically insulating conformal layer, so hydrogel moisture can't short the I²C pins while heat still passes freely. Stage 4 — the most critical layer for accuracy — caps the top and sides with dense foam, forcing the sensor to measure heat radiating from below rather than the hospital's air conditioning.

PCB layout rules for honest readings

  • 01Zero self-heating. One-shot mode at 1 Hz: wake, draw ~3.5 µA for a single reading, drop to 150 nA sleep — so the chip never warms its own data.
  • 02Pull-up isolation. SDA/SCL pull-up resistors sit 5–10 mm away, since current through them dissipates heat.
  • 03Decoupling cap. A 0.1 µF bypass capacitor next to V+/GND filters power-supply noise from the ESP32's Bluetooth radio.
  • 04Thermal relief. Traces are kept as thin as safe so they can't act as heatsinks and pull heat away from the sensor.
