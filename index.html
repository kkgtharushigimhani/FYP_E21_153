Project Overview
Smart Wound Monitoring System

This is our EE405 undergraduate project portfolio. The main idea is to build a low-cost smart plaster that can monitor important wound-bed changes without repeatedly opening the dressing.

IWMTAS · Final Year Project

Intelligent Wound Monitoring & Therapy Adjustment System

pH monitoring · temperature monitoring · moisture/exudate monitoring · disposable patch + reusable electronics
Problem

Chronic and diabetic wounds can become worse under a dressing before clear visible signs appear. Repeated dressing removal can also disturb healing and increase contamination risk.

Our Idea

We are developing a smart wound plaster that tracks hidden wound-bed trends and supports early clinical warning instead of depending only on visual inspection.

Selected Parameters

From the review work, we selected pH, temperature and moisture/exudate as the main practical parameters for first-stage monitoring.

System Design

The wound-contact sensing part is planned as disposable, while the DAQ electronics and main communication unit are kept reusable to reduce cost.

Review Foundation

We used a literature-derived dataset and sensor matrix to justify the selected sensing parameters and guide the design direction.

Patch Concept

The disposable strip-like plaster is intended to sense local wound regions while remaining thin, flexible and suitable for early prototype testing.

Clinical Direction

The final output should show trends such as stable, watch, inspect or escalate so that clinicians can make the final decision.

Portfolio Focus

This website documents the weekly progress from literature review and biological background study to sensor selection, statistical analysis, circuit planning, cost estimation, component orders and early fabrication preparation.

Research Article Review · Paper 1

Clinical Significance of Wound Temperature Monitoring in Diabetic Foot Microcirculation Reconstruction

Hong Liu, Xian-Yan Yan, Guo-Qing Li, Bao-Na Wang, Dong Wang, Yong-Hong Zhang, Jin-Li Guo
Clinical Background

Chronic hyperglycemia causes vascular endothelial damage, leading to micro-thrombosis and microcirculation failure. 25% of diabetic patients develop foot ulcers; 50–60% progress to chronic non-healing wounds without precise intervention.

Study Methodology

Retrospective analysis of 29 patients (Wagner grade ≥ 3). Infrared temperature recorded at wound center and distal tibia (5 cm above ankle) at admission, weekly for 1 month, then monthly. Room temperature controlled at 20–25°C.

Treatment Applied

Transverse Tibial Bone Transport via distraction histogenesis stimulated neurovascular regeneration. Ultrasound debridement cleared biofilms. Silver-containing lipid hydrocolloid dressings maintained the wound microenvironment.

Relevance to IWMTAS

This paper directly defines our core monitoring logic. The 33°C threshold will be programmed as a critical alert in our system. The Equilibrium Point concept (P>0.05 between wound center and distal tibia) becomes our primary success metric, and the multi-site, multi-interval monitoring protocol forms the backbone of our sensor array design.

Technical Report Review · Paper 2

Topical Radiant Heating (TRH) in Wound Healing — Experimental Study in a Donor Site Wound Model

Warm-Up™ Dressing System (Arizant Healthcare) · Laser Doppler Imaging Study
What is TRH?

Topical Radiant Heating delivers constant radiant heat at 38°C via the Warm-Up™ non-contact dressing. It maintains a humid microenvironment to facilitate re-epithelialization and collagen synthesis via Van't Hoff's Law and oxygen-dependent enzyme activity.

Study Design

Study 1: n=20 healthy volunteers — intermittent TRH on right thigh, left as control.
Study 2: n=12 skin graft patients — 6 received TRH, 6 standard dressings. Protocol: 3× 1-hour heating cycles over 5 hours at 38°C.

Measurement Tools

Laser Doppler Imaging (helium-neon 632.8 nm) mapped dermal microvascular blood flow. Immunohistochemical analysis tracked NP57 (neutrophils), CD3 (T-lymphocytes), CD68 (macrophages) via Streptavidin-Biotin staining.

Smart wound dressing concept — continuous monitoring of temperature and moisture within a flexible bandage (from related literature).
Smart bandage system architecture showing sensor integration, wireless communication, and clinical diagnostics methodology.

Van't Hoff's Law

Applied heat at 38°C accelerates biochemical reactions — hyperthermia stimulates fibroblast proliferation and enhances polymorph chemotaxis and phagocytosis.

Oxygen Tension Elevation

TRH increases subcutaneous oxygen tension, enabling oxygen-dependent collagen cross-linking via prolyl 4-hydroxylase. Oxygen elevation persists up to 3 hours post-treatment.

Immune Regulatory Effect

Early lymphocytic infiltrate regulates neutrophil activity, preventing chaotic cytokine release — associated with superior healing prognosis.

Temporal Blood Flow Dynamics

Blood flow returns to baseline within 1 hour of TRH cessation. Subcutaneous oxygen tension remains elevated for up to 3 hours — informs therapy scheduling.

Relevance to IWMTAS

TRH becomes our primary therapy module. The 38°C target, 1-hour cycle protocol, and 3-hour oxygen elevation window will directly shape our adaptive therapy scheduling algorithm. The LDI-validated blood flow response provides the scientific basis for using temperature as a real-time therapy response indicator.

Academic Report: Physiological Mechanisms of Wound Healing & Role of Thermal Monitoring

Resource Book Analysis — Ratnatunga Clinical Framework
Tissue Restitution

Re-establishment of epithelial continuity via migration of existing cells — occurs before proliferation and inflammation. Example: gastric erosion recovery.

Tissue Regeneration

Replacement of lost cells with the same cell type. Requires intact Basement Membrane (BM) and surviving stem cells. Labile and stable cells can regenerate.

Tissue Repair (Scarring)

When BM is destroyed — damaged tissue is replaced by fibrous scar tissue through granulation tissue formation and collagen deposition.

Sensor Technology for Wound Monitoring: TMP117 Architecture, Size & Application

Texas Instruments TMP117 · Flexible Bioelectronic Systems Research
TMP117 sensor operation, size specifications, and wound bandage integration methodology.
Smart bandage architecture — flexible substrate integration, biocompatible encapsulation, wireless mapping, and clinical diagnostic application.
How TMP117 Works

Uses a thermal BJT coupled with a 16-bit ADC for digital conversion. Operates at 3.5 µA (1-Hz cycle) and 150 nA in shutdown mode — prevents self-heating. Outputs via I2C/SMBus. Averaging mode (8 readings) reduces thermal noise to achieve ±0.1°C clinical-grade accuracy.

Physical Dimensions

WSON Package: 2.00 mm × 2.00 mm
DSBGA Package: 1.53 mm × 1.00 mm (grain of sand)
Array Thickness: 12–50 µm on polyimide substrate — thinner than a strand of human hair.

Smart Bandage Integration

Sensors mounted on Flexible PCB (FPCB) to conform to wound topography. Sealed in medical-grade silicone (PDMS) for biocompatibility. Wireless via BLE/NFC. Array creates continuous thermal map — hot spots = infection, cold spots = ischemia.

FPCB sensing layer · TCA9548A I2C multiplexer · Multi-point wound temperature mapping
Distributed Sensor Concept

The TMP117 was selected as the main temperature sensor and arranged as a multi-point sensor array. This allows the patch to compare temperature across the wound center, wound edge and surrounding periwound region instead of relying on one single point.

I2C Expansion Method

Because TMP117 has limited address options, the TCA9548A I2C multiplexer was included to separate the sensor nodes into independent channels and avoid address conflicts when many sensors are used.

FPCB Patch Direction

The Week 4 design focused on a flexible sensor layer that can be placed close to the wound area, while passivation and insulation layers protect the electronics from wound moisture and external temperature disturbance.

Biochemical Monitoring Study · Week 5

pH Change from Wound Corner to Center

Corner/periwound pH · Wound edge pH · Wound center alkalinity · Infection risk indication
Corner / Periwound Region

The wound corner and surrounding skin are usually more acidic and closer to normal skin condition. This region supports the skin barrier and gives us a useful starting point to understand healing-cell movement.

Wound Edge / Margin

The wound edge is the active healing boundary. A mildly acidic condition supports epithelial cell migration, enzyme balance and tissue repair from the wound edge toward the center.

Wound Center / Bed

The wound center can become more alkaline, especially when exudate, dead tissue, bacterial activity, inflammation and protease activity increase. This can be a warning sign for delayed healing.

Bacterial Metabolism

Bacteria can produce alkaline by-products, increasing wound pH and supporting biofilm formation.

Necrotic Tissue

Dead tissue and cellular debris disturb the wound microenvironment and can contribute to alkalinity.

Inflammation

Long-term inflammation changes the biochemical environment and may indicate poor healing progression.

Protease Activity

Excess protease activity can break down newly formed tissue and slow epithelialization.

Alert Threshold Concept

pH above 7.1 at the center can be treated as a warning condition for chronic or infected wound behavior.
