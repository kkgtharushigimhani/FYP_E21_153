Week 4
FPCB Temperature Sensor Architecture & Wound Stage Mapping

Week 4 converted the literature and sensor selection work into a practical smart wound patch architecture using a distributed TMP117 temperature sensor array, FPCB design rules, wound-stage interpretation and project funding preparation.

Architecture Design · Week 4

Distributed TMP117 Temperature Sensor Array for Smart Wound Monitoring

FPCB sensing layer · TCA9548A I2C multiplexer · Multi-point wound temperature mapping
Distributed Sensor Concept

The TMP117 was selected as the main temperature sensor and arranged as a multi-point sensor array. This allows the patch to compare temperature across the wound center, wound edge and surrounding periwound region instead of relying on one single point.

I2C Expansion Method

Because TMP117 has limited address options, the TCA9548A I2C multiplexer was included to separate the sensor nodes into independent channels and avoid address conflicts when many sensors are used.

FPCB Patch Direction

The Week 4 design focused on a flexible sensor layer that can be placed close to the wound area, while passivation and insulation layers protect the electronics from wound moisture and external temperature disturbance.

TMP117 as Main Sensor

Selected because it provides high temperature accuracy with a simple digital I2C interface and low power consumption for wearable patch use.

TCA9548A Multiplexer

Used to connect more TMP117 sensors than the normal address limit allows, making multi-region monitoring possible.

Self-heating Reduction

One-shot sampling, low current operation and careful placement of pull-up resistors were considered to reduce sensor self-heating error.

FPCB Routing Rules

Thin traces, local decoupling and stable sensor placement were considered for reliable flexible patch fabrication.

Wound Stage Mapping

Hemostasis, inflammation, proliferation and remodeling were linked with possible temperature changes during healing.

Fund Request Prepared

The project fund request was prepared for prototype fabrication, materials, electronic components and FPCB-related work.

Wound Healing Stage Understanding
Wound healing stages were reviewed to understand how temperature and healing activity can vary from wound edge to center.
TCA9548A design reference used to support multiple I2C sensor branches in the IWMTAS architecture.
Relevance to IWMTAS

Week 4 is the bridge between research and implementation. It converts the selected temperature sensor into a real multi-sensor patch architecture. This allows IWMTAS to detect local wound changes and compare different wound regions, which is more useful than a single temperature reading.

Week 5
pH Gradient in Wound Healing

Week 5 extended the monitoring concept from temperature only to biochemical wound condition monitoring by studying how pH changes from the wound corner toward the wound center.

Biochemical Monitoring Study · Week 5

pH Change from Wound Corner to Center

Corner/periwound pH · Wound edge pH · Wound center alkalinity · Infection risk indication
Corner / Periwound Region

The wound corner and surrounding skin are usually more acidic and closer to normal skin condition. This region supports skin barrier protection and provides the starting area for healing cell migration.

Wound Edge / Margin

The wound edge is the active healing boundary. A mildly acidic condition supports epithelial cell migration, enzyme balance and tissue repair from the wound edge toward the center.

Wound Center / Bed

The wound center can become more alkaline, especially when exudate, dead tissue, bacterial activity, inflammation and protease activity increase. This can be a warning sign for delayed healing.

Wound Center / BedMore alkalinepH > 7.1Possible delayed healing, biofilm formation, infection or tissue breakdown risk

Main Week 5 idea: from wound corner to wound center, pH generally increases. A rising alkaline center can be used as an early warning feature in future IWMTAS monitoring.

Exudate Collection

The wound center often collects more wound fluid, proteins, enzymes, inflammatory cells and waste materials.

Bacterial Metabolism

Bacteria can produce alkaline by-products, increasing wound pH and supporting biofilm formation.

Necrotic Tissue

Dead tissue and cellular debris disturb the wound microenvironment and can contribute to alkalinity.

Inflammation

Long-term inflammation changes the biochemical environment and may indicate poor healing progression.

Protease Activity

Excess protease activity can break down newly formed tissue and slow epithelialization.

Alert Threshold Concept

pH above 7.1 at the center can be treated as a warning condition for chronic or infected wound behavior.

Relevance to IWMTAS

Week 5 adds a second monitoring dimension to the project. Temperature can indicate perfusion and inflammation, while pH can indicate biochemical healing condition. Combining both in future work can improve early detection of infection risk, delayed healing and abnormal wound-bed conditions.
