Week 1

Literature Review - Clinical Foundation

During the first week, the work focused on understanding diabetic foot ulcer healing, wound temperature monitoring and the clinical value of controlled thermal therapy.

Open Week 1 Drive Folder

Paper 1

Wound temperature as a microcirculation indicator

The review studied wound temperature monitoring at different anatomical sites in diabetic foot patients undergoing microcirculation reconstruction. The main engineering lesson was that wound surface temperature can act as a non-invasive proxy for blood perfusion, angiogenesis and healing progression.

  • Tracked wound-center and distal-tibia temperatures over time.
  • Identified temperature convergence as a sign of improved microcirculation.
  • Highlighted 33°C as a critical biological threshold for healing activity.
  • Connected temperature rise with microvascular density and tissue repair.

Paper 2

Topical radiant heating and wound repair

The second review investigated topical radiant heating at 38°C using a non-contact dressing system. This paper helped clarify how controlled heat can increase dermal blood flow, support immune response and improve the wound-healing environment.

Outcome

Week 1 contribution to the project

Clinical logic

Temperature is not used only as a number; it is interpreted as a signal related to perfusion, inflammation and wound repair.

Alert concept

Low temperature or abnormal temperature differences can be used to flag delayed healing and possible intervention need.

Design requirement

The future patch must measure temperature accurately while avoiding ambient temperature interference.
