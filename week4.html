New Week 4 Work

Distributed TMP117 temperature sensor array

A robust multi-node architecture was designed around the TMP117 digital temperature sensor. Since each TMP117 supports only four I²C addresses, the architecture uses a TCA9548A I²C multiplexer to expand address capacity and support multiple sensor nodes.

  • TCA9548A enables up to eight independent downstream I²C channels.
  • Each channel can read multiple TMP117 sensors with different ADD0 address settings.
  • Four physical wound/patch regions are considered for spatial temperature monitoring.
  • Wired differential I²C using PCA9615 or wireless ESP-NOW region modules are considered.

4-stage patch structure

Temperature sensing architecture in the smart wound patch

Stage 1

Wound interface

Thin medical-grade substrate or hydrocolloid layer transfers heat from the wound bed to the sensor with minimal thermal lag.

Stage 2

Flex PCB sensor layer

TMP117 is mounted on the FPCB at the wound region of interest with a localized thermal pad and careful trace routing.

Stage 3

Hydrogel and passivation

Protective passivation prevents short-circuiting and wet exudate contact while maintaining thermal coupling.

Stage 4

Ambient shielding

PU or silicone foam encapsulation blocks external room temperature from biasing the wound temperature reading.

PCB and firmware rules

Accuracy protection strategies

Week 4 also defined the design rules needed to keep the sensor reading clinically useful. The TMP117 is operated in one-shot mode to reduce self-heating. Pull-up resistors are placed away from the sensor, a 0.1 µF bypass capacitor is placed near the sensor supply pins, and thin traces reduce heat-sinking effects.

Wound healing understanding

Corner-to-center healing progression

Hemostasis

Blood clot and provisional matrix form immediately after injury.

Inflammation

Immune cells clear bacteria and debris; heat and swelling may rise.

Proliferation

Granulation tissue, angiogenesis and epithelial migration move from edge toward center.

Remodeling

Collagen reorganizes and wound strength gradually improves.

The Week 4 wound-stage study emphasized that healing is spatially different across the periwound, wound edge and wound bed. For this reason, a distributed temperature sensing layout is more useful than a single isolated point when evaluating wound progression.

Funding update

Prototype fund request prepared

The fund request describes the project as the development of an ultra-low-cost, medical-grade flexible smart wound monitoring patch for diabetic foot ulcers.

  • Project No.: 36
  • Budget request: LKR 250,000 for 10 FPCB prototypes
  • Focus: electronic components, nanomaterials, biocompatible materials and PCB fabrication services
  • Initial prototype stage uses simulated wound readings, not personal patient data.

Expected Week 4 deliverables

Fabricated flexible disposable sensing patch concept, reusable data-acquisition module, calibrated laboratory test setup and early alert logic for abnormal wound conditions.
